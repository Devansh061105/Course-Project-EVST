Biodiversity and Ecosystem Productivity

Does biodiversity track ecosystem productivity once the uneven effort behind citizen-science records is corrected? Bird, plant and butterfly richness from GBIF against satellite productivity, on a one-degree grid over South and Southeast Asia.

3D terrain map The region as terrain, coloured by vegetation, with a richness column per cell. Take the guided tour or click any place for its record and its most-recorded species. Flat atlas Fifteen layers, from raw and corrected richness to model residuals, local slopes, survey gaps and protected land, with a linked richness-against-productivity scatter.

What the analysis found

  • Raw species counts mostly map survey effort (r = 0.81). After correction that falls to r = 0.24.
  • Across regions, richer cells are more productive (coefficient +0.27 after climate). Inside an ecoregion the link nearly disappears (+0.03 to +0.06).
  • Plants and butterflies show the same pattern as birds.
  • The relationship is a straight line, not a hump, and the data cannot say which way it runs.
  • Bird records are close to complete across India, Nepal, Thailand and Sri Lanka, and thin in western Pakistan, upland Myanmar and the Tibetan margin.
